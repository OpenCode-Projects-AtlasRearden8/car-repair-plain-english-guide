# Maintenance Primer

You don't need to become a mechanic. You need roughly **a page and a half of knowledge**, applied on schedule, to avoid most surprise repair bills. This is that page and a half.

## The three kinds of car parts

Almost everything on your car falls into one of these:

| Type | Behavior | Your job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Consumables** | Wear out on a schedule (oil, filters, brake pads, wiper blades, tires, batteries) | Replace on or near the interval — this is where the surprising-big-bills get prevented |
| **Fluids** | Degrade chemically (oil, coolant, brake fluid, transmission fluid, power steering in older cars) | Same: interval-based. Old fluid is quietly corrosive |
| **Wear-eventual parts** | Fail somewhat predictably *late* in life (belts, hoses, ignition coils, water pumps, suspension bushings) | Watch, don't panic. Replace *at* the interval, not years early, and not 20,000 miles past it |

The myth to drop: there's no such thing as "I never have to maintain it if I'm careful." Modern cars just fail *later and more quietly* than old ones.

## The three sources that outrank every article on the internet

1. **Your owner's manual.** Written by people who built your specific car. Contains your real maintenance schedule and fluid specs. Free. Check the glovebox or the manufacturer's site.
2. **The sticker in your door jamb.** Tire pressure and load ratings for *your exact car* — often different from the number printed on the tire itself (which is the tire's max, not your car's recommended pressure).
3. **The maintenance light/memory** in many modern dashboards, plus one third-party maintenance schedule source if the manual is missing. Enough; ignore the internet's fear.

## The intervals worth memorizing

Intervals vary by make, model, and driving style (severe driving = lots of short trips, towing, ultra-hot or ultra-cold climates = shorter intervals). These are typical modern numbers; **your manual wins**:

| Item | Typical modern interval | What happens if ignored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Engine oil + filter** | 5,000–10,000 mi (synthetic) — short-trip city drivers: closer to 5,000 | Sludge; eventually bearing/engine damage — the expensive kind |
| **Engine air filter** | ~15,000–30,000 mi, eyeball it | Worse performance and economy; cheap and easy DIY |
| **Cabin air filter** | ~12,000–15,000 mi | Stale/musty air; airflow drops |
| **Brake fluid** | Every 2 years (yes, time-based — it absorbs moisture) | Milky fluid; corroded brake internals; soft pedal |
| **Coolant** | Long-life coolants: ~5 yr / 100k mi initially | Corrosion, water pump death, overheating |
| **Spark plugs** | Iridium: often 60k–120k mi | Misfires (the P0300 family), worse economy |
| **Automatic transmission fluid** | Varies wildly: ~60k–150k mi, some "lifetime" (lifetime = *not really*) | Harsh shifts, early transmission death |
| **Brake pads/rotors** | 30k–70k mi depending on driving style | Metal-on-metal = destroyed rotors ($$$) |
| **Tires** | Wear bars, or ~6 years max by age even with tread left | Blowouts; poor wet braking |
| **Battery** | ~3–6 years, climate-dependent | No-start; charging system strain |
| **Wiper blades** | 6–12 months | Amusing until it's raining sideways |
| **Timing belt** (belt-driven engines only) | ~60k–105k mi | Snaps → often destroys the engine entirely. Engines with **timing chains** don't need this interval |
| **Serpentine/aux belt** | Inspect ~60k, replace ~90k–105k | Snaps → car stops, generally not catastrophic |

## The 30-minute monthly walk-around

Once a month, while gassing up or before a drive:

1. **Tires:** look for damage, uneven wear, objects. Check pressures with a $10 gauge (door jamb sticker's numbers, not the tire's max).
2. **Lights:** walk around the car while a helper (or a garage wall reflection) shows you brake lights and turn signals.
3. **Fluids (pop the hood):** oil level and color, coolant level in the reservoir (do *not* open a hot radiator cap), brake fluid level and clarity, washer fluid.
4. **Brakes:** any squeal, grind, pulsation, or pulls? Listen on a quiet stretch of road, gently, at low speed.
5. **Under the car (look, don't crawl):** fresh spots on the driveway where it was parked. Sweet = coolant, dark = oil, red/brown = transmission or power steering fluid, clear water near the front on a humid day after A/C = normal condensation, ignore it.
6. **Battery terminals:** any fuzzy white/green crust = worth a rinse and cleaning (cheap, 10 minutes, or free at most parts stores).

Ten minutes monthly beats three random dead-battery Saturdays a year.

## Free/cheap DIY wins

Rough difficulty staircase — start at the bottom:

- **Wiper blades, cabin filter, engine air filter:** genuinely easy, plenty of 5-minute videos per exact model.
- **Battery, bulbs:** easy-to-moderate, model-specific; check YouTube once.
- **Oil changes:** moderate; you need a wrench, a drain pan, a jack and stands (never crawl under a car supported only by a jack), gloves, and the right filter. Worth learning — but *never worth injuring yourself over; a $40 quick lube is not a scam.*
- **Tire rotation, spare-tire practice:** rotation is easy if you're comfortable jacking the car; also, your spare has a jack *and instructions in the trunk* — find them before you need them, in daylight.
- **Brakes deep-cleanse, sensors, serpentine belt:** you'll want tools, a service manual for your model, and a free afternoon.

General DIY rule: **any job where a mistake means losing brakes or being stranded** = pay a pro the modest fee.

## Driving habits that cost (or save) real money

- **Replace the habit of "warm it up by idling":** modern cars want 30 seconds, then gentle driving; idling cold actually increases wear.
- **Gentle first mile** beats worming the throttle after startup. Timers don't lie: oil takes ~1–2 min to fully circulate.
- **Don't ride the brakes downhill**; downshift (or in an automatic, use the brake, lift-off pattern) so the brakes don't glaze and cook.
- **Fix small oil/coolant leaks early.** Low fluid is a slow-motion engine killer.
- **Don't ride on old gas that sits for a year**; get a cheap fuel-safe gas can or just refill when storage ends.
- **Replace the gas cap until it clicks** (remember those P0440 codes? ~1 minute fix, sometimes free).

## Quick glossary for the above that you'll hear at a shop

- **Alignment:** the wheels' angles relative to each other and the road. Knocked out by potholes; causes uneven tire wear and pull. ~$100–150 a shop.
- **Balancing:** evenly distributing wheel weight so it doesn't vibrate at highway speed. With rotation.
- **Flat rate:** see [Decode the Estimate](estimates.md) — published hours-per-job tables.
- **Torque spec:** how tight a bolt must be. This is why "just snug it" by feel is where amateur brake jobs go wrong.
- **Multimeter/battery tester:** a $15 battery tester that plugs into the OBD port does the check most importantly for battery+alternator.
