# Car Repair, in Plain English

**For drivers who dread the service desk.**

You are not stupid. The estimate is written in code — literal P0301 codes, plus shop jargon like "flat rate," "core charge," and "shop supplies." This guide decodes it, step by step, never condescending, the way a friend would explain it over coffee.

## The honest premise of this whole guide

1. **Most repair bills are fair.** Shops have overhead, lifts, insurance, and trained people. You are paying for expertise you don't have, and that's a fair trade.
2. **Some line items are confusing by habit, not malice.** But confusion is expensive for you either way, so we remove it.
3. **Education, never diagnosis.** We teach you to ask better questions and understand the answers. We never tell you what's wrong with *your specific car*. Only a professional who can see, hear, and touch the car can do that.

## How to use this guide

- **In the parking lot, staring at an estimate?** Jump to [Decode the Estimate](estimates.md) and [Confident Questions](questions.md).
- **Check engine light just came on?** Start with [Understand OBD-II Codes](obd-codes.md).
- **Got a big quote and a sinking feeling?** Use [AI Quote Translator](ai-translator.md) to break it down before deciding.
- **Want fewer surprise repairs next year?** Read [Maintenance Primer](maintenance-primer.md) once. Seriously, once.

## Tools you'll want on your phone

- A cheap Bluetooth **OBD-II reader** (a generic ELM327-style dongle) plus a free app can *read* codes for you. This is optional — many auto parts stores and shops will read codes for free.
- The **owner's manual** for your car. It's free (check the glovebox, or find the official PDF from the manufacturer if yours is missing). It contains your real maintenance schedule.
- Nothing else. This guide requires no paid subscriptions and no accounts.

## Safety first, always

Brakes, steering, tires, and anything that affects whether the car stops or steers are **never** "wait and see" items. If a professional says those are unsafe, fix that first, even if it hurts. Everything else can usually wait a bit, negotiate, or get a second opinion.
